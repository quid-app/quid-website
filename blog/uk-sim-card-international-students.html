A UK SIM in your first hour, a better one in your first month.

For international students in the UK

QBy the Quid team · 3 min read · sourced and datedChecked 7 November 2026

A UK mobile number is the first thing the NHS, your bank and your university will ask for, so this is a day-one job. It is also the one where new arrivals overpay for months. The fix is to treat it as two decisions.

On this page

On this page
01

Short answer

Short answer

Buy a pay-as-you-go SIM on arrival: no bank account, no credit check, no address. Keep your home SIM working as an eSIM if your phone supports it. Once you have a UK bank account, move to a rolling SIM-only plan from one of the smaller networks that run on EE, O2, Three or Vodafone; they are cheaper and you can leave monthly. Avoid 12 or 24-month contracts in year one. Check coverage at your accommodation on Ofcom's signal checker before you commit.

02

Decision one: the arrival SIM

Pay-as-you-go needs nothing but a phone. You top up credit or buy a bundle of data, minutes and texts. Supermarkets and phone shops sell them; some networks will post a free SIM to your home country before you travel, as UCL notes, so you can land with a working number.

Make sure your phone is unlocked before you leave home. St Andrews' test is simple: put in a SIM from another network and try a call. If it fails, your home provider has to unlock it, and that is far easier to arrange from home.

03

Keep your home number

Your family, your home bank and your home two-factor codes all use your old number. UK iPhones and most Android phones take one physical SIM plus an eSIM, so convert either your home SIM or your UK SIM to an eSIM and run both. UCL's advice is to convert the home SIM to an eSIM before travelling, because not every UK provider offers eSIM on pay-as-you-go. Turn off data roaming on the home SIM the moment you land; roaming data on an overseas contract is where the first big bill comes from.

04

Decision two: the real plan

Once you have a UK bank account, move to a SIM-only plan. UCL's example is £6 a month for 5GB; more money buys more data. The things to check:

  • Rolling monthly or fixed term. Some SIM-only plans lock you in for 12 months. Rolling plans let you leave or change each month, which matters if you move accommodation or go home for summer.
  • Who runs the network. The UK has four physical networks (EE, O2, Three, Vodafone). Everyone else rents one of them and is usually cheaper for the same coverage. Pick by coverage at your postcode using Ofcom's mobile signal checker, not by brand.
  • Credit checks. Pay-monthly plans with a handset almost always run a credit check, which you will fail with no UK history. SIM-only rolling plans paid by card or Direct Debit are the way round it.
  • International calls. If you call home often, check the per-minute rate or the add-on bundle; it varies more than anything else between providers.
05

Switching later

Keeping your UK number when you change network is a text message: ask your current provider for a PAC code and give it to the new one. Leaving without keeping the number is a STAC code. Both are free.

06

Numbers to save

999 (or 112) for emergencies; both work from any SIM. 111 for non-emergency NHS advice and 101 for non-emergency police; these need a UK SIM or a roaming agreement that supports UK calls. St Andrews warns that on a foreign SIM, emergency services may not be able to call you back if the line drops.

Quid's checklist puts the SIM on day one and reminds you to review the plan once the bank account is open. The Deals page lists current student SIM offers.

07

Quick answers

Can an international student get a UK SIM without a bank account?

Yes. Pay-as-you-go SIMs need no bank account, credit check or address.

Do I need a credit check for a UK phone contract?

Pay-monthly contracts with a handset usually run one. SIM-only rolling plans paid by card generally do not.

Can I keep my home phone number in the UK?

Yes, by running your home SIM as an eSIM alongside a UK SIM, with data roaming switched off.

Which UK mobile network is best for students?

Whichever has the best coverage at your accommodation. Check your postcode on Ofcom's signal checker; the smaller brands use the same four networks.
